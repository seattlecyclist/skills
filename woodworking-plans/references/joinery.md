# Joinery rules of thumb

Starting proportions for sizing joints and for the review. They are conventions from shop practice,
not engineering limits; depart from them on purpose and say why.

## Mortise and tenon
- Tenon thickness about 1/3 of the stock (1/2" on 1-1/2", 1/4" to 3/8" on 3/4"). Up to 1/2 when the mortised part is much thicker.
- Mortise walls at least 3/16", better 1/4" or more. Mortise at least 1/2" from the end of a leg, or the end grain blows out (use a haunch).
- Tenon length: 1" to 1-1/2" in legs; about 2/3 of the mortised part's width; 3/4" minimum for stub tenons.
- Tenon width no more than about 5× its thickness; split wide tenons into twins.
- Shoulders: 1/4" to 1/2" on the edges so the mortise has walls.
- Drawbore pegs: 3/8" in 1-1/2" stock, offset 1/32" to 1/16".

## Castle joint
- Two crossing slots in the post top, each the thickness of the rail that drops in, depth equal to the rail height. The rails half-lap each other inside the post.
- Towers (the four corners left) at least 1/4 of the post width, and at least 3/4". Long grain, so strong once captured, fragile while cutting.
- The lower rail is notched from the top, the upper from the bottom; rail tops finish flush with the post top.
- The rail centerline runs through the post center, so the inner quarter of the post stands inside the rail line.
- Holds by gravity; add one bolt or dowel down through the lap to keep the upper rail from lifting.
- Castle slots are deep (6-1/2" in a bed post): cut the cheeks on the bandsaw and chop or rout the waste. A dado stack can't reach.
- If anything bolts into a rail end beyond the post (bed bolts into a headboard), the overhang past the post must hold the barrel nut clear of the post: about 3" for a nut 2-1/4" from the end.

## Bridle (open slot)
- Slot 1/3 of the post thickness, cheeks at least 3/8".
- Slot depth equal to the rail height; peg or glue.

## Half-lap
- Each part cut to half its thickness at the crossing; glue area is the full overlap.
- For corner laps on frames, add a screw or peg if the frame racks.

## Dado, groove, rabbet
- Dado depth 1/3 of the panel thickness in solid wood, 1/4" to 3/8" in 3/4" plywood.
- Groove for a floating panel: 1/4" wide, 3/8" to 1/2" deep; the panel 1/16" to 1/8" short of the groove bottom on each side (more across the grain of solid panels; see movement).
- Rabbet for a back: as deep as the back is thick, 3/8" to 1/2" wide.

## Dovetails
- Slope 1:6 to 1:8 in hardwood (1:6 softwood). Pins at least 1/4" at the narrow end.
- Half-pins at both edges.

## Dowels, biscuits, dominoes
- Dowel diameter about 1/2 the stock thickness; at least two per joint; 1" into each part.
- These align and add some glue area. They are not a substitute for a tenon in a chair or bed.

## Screws and bolts
- Screws into end grain hold about half as well; don't hang load on them.
- Screw length: 2× to 3× the thickness of the part being fastened.
- Bed bolts 3/8" × 6" with barrel nuts 2" to 2-1/2" from the rail end; two per joint, spaced vertically, or the joint hinges.
- Cross dowels (barrel nuts) in a post: at least 1" from the end and centered in the thickness.

## Wood movement
- Solid wood moves across the grain about 1/4" per 12" of width, seasonally (more in flatsawn, less in quartersawn).
- Never glue a long-grain part across the width of a solid panel wider than about 6". Use buttons, figure-8 fasteners, slotted holes, breadboard ends glued only in the middle, or floating panels.
- Plywood and MDF don't move; glue them anywhere.

## Spans and sizes (starting points)
- Bed side rails: 1-1/4" × 6" minimum in hardwood with a center support for queen and king; 1-1/2" preferred.
- Shelves of 3/4" hardwood: 32" max span for books; 3/4" plywood: 28" (add a front edge strip to stiffen).
- Table aprons: 3" to 4" tall; legs at least 1-3/4" square for dining tables.
- Seat height 17" to 18"; table height 29" to 30"; desk 28" to 30"; counter 36"; bench 18".
