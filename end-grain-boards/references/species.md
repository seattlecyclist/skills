# Species

`scripts/species.py` holds the numbers (end-grain colour for drawings, Janka hardness, tangential and
radial shrinkage, notes). Keys: maple, walnut, cherry, white-oak, ash, purpleheart, padauk, jatoba,
yellowheart, sapele, bloodwood, wenge, yellow-birch, beech, canarywood, teak. Unknown names are
allowed with a warning; give them a colour with `"species_colors": {"role": "#rrggbb"}`.

## Choosing
- **Hardness:** 900 lbf Janka and up for a working surface. Hard maple (1450) is the benchmark;
  walnut (1010) and cherry (950) are fine and are the classic darks.
- **Pores:** closed or small pores. Red oak's open pores run straight through end grain: refused.
- **Food safety and health:** no spalted wood (decay), no cocobolo (sensitiser); wenge splinters
  fester, so keep it to accents. Padauk dust irritates and its colour bleeds into maple: wipe the
  faces with acetone before gluing.
- **Movement:** end-grain boards move across both directions of the face. Species with similar
  tangential shrinkage stay flatter. The check warns past 4 points apart (maple 9.9% with padauk
  5.2%); those pairs are common and work, but acclimate them together and keep the board oiled.

## Contrast for patterns
- 3D cubes need three clear values: light (maple, ash, yellow birch), mid (cherry, sapele, padauk,
  canarywood), dark (walnut, purpleheart, wenge as an accent). Cherry darkens over months, closing
  the gap to walnut; padauk browns too. Say so when you pick them.
- Two-wood patterns (stars, checker, brick): one light, one dark. Maple and walnut is the classic.
- Bright exotics (purpleheart, padauk, yellowheart, bloodwood) shift toward brown with UV; a UV
  inhibiting oil slows it.

## Buying
- Rhombus and triangle bars need blanks under 1-1/4" thick for sides up to 1-1/2", so 6/4 or 5/4
  rough stock. Square bars for checkerboards need 8/4 for 1-1/2" squares.
- The plan's board feet add 25% for defects and milling; buy a little more of the wood the pattern
  uses most.
